<objective>
Bind ACP turn completion to the live backend turn, bound silent turns with a no-progress watchdog, and recover orphaned backend turns, so a stopped or failed turn can never falsely complete its successor and a retry storm can no longer hang the client.

Purpose: the 2026-11-25 field incident had three independent defects — a new turn was falsely completed by a prior turn's late idle/terminal signal (~4 s in), the wrapper forwarded 867 client retries into a still-running backend turn for 79 minutes, and a 429 retry backoff could keep a turn silent for ~50 minutes with no progress bound. The fix adds three independent, test-locked defenses at the machine and front tiers without changing the wire vocabulary, the single-active-turn invariant, or the bypass-lane stop primitive.

Output: turn-scoped terminal/idle binding with forensic logging, a configurable no-progress watchdog that resolves an honest coded failure, and a bounded stop-plus-one-retry orphan recovery that turns a permanent session-busy loop into either a recovered turn or a non-retryable error.
</objective>

Design decisions and their rationale:

- Retired turnId: `retiredTurnID` persists across turns. It is set when a turn resolves with a bound turnId and when a late terminal observed after resolution names the resolved turn. An unbound new turn ignores any terminal/turn-started whose turnId equals the retired ID. A terminal or turn-start with an EMPTY turnId never binds and never resolves; the quiet window remains the honest fallback (all shipped carriers are turnId-scoped per dialect §5).
- Idle binding: the running patch records the maximum revision (and its reason). An idle patch completes only when both revisions are present and the idle revision is strictly greater. A missing revision on either side is the strongest conservative fallback: the patch is observation only (it cannot prove order), and the turn resolves through its terminal, quiet window, or watchdog. While cancelling, any leaving-running patch still resolves cancelled (the stop was explicit and the patch is the stop's outcome); while running, a `cancelled` status or `session_stopped` reason is another turn's stop outcome and is ignored. Revisions are per-session monotonic in the captures (`completion-idle-patch` 1->2, `lingering-latch-32010` 1->2 and 3->4), so the ordering rule is sound for the observed corpus.
- Watchdog: progress is deliberately narrow (the scope list). The watchdog is a running-turn guard only: a cancelling turn keeps the existing cancel/quiet semantics (the stop was explicit — the watchdog never fires and never sends a second stop). On expiry it sends session/stop on the bypass lane BEFORE resolving, so no new prompt can acquire the slot and race a late stop against its own send; it then resolves `StopError/SignalStall` with a recorded error. The front reads `Resolution().Err` to produce the coded -32603 message. Defaults are 15 min base and 30 min while a tool is active; an explicit base override without an explicit tool override scales the tool budget to 2x base (test ergonomics). The configuration seam is `acpfront.WithTurnStallTimeout` (the Front/turn option allowed by the scope); no env/CLI knob is added in this quick task.
- Orphan recovery: the machine cannot see other machines, so it cannot by itself distinguish a backend orphan from a legitimate local concurrent turn. The front-wide slot proves no local turn is active while its prompt holds the slot; therefore orphan recovery is an opt-in machine flag that the Front enables on every machine it registers for prompts — `NewSession` and `resumeSessionLocked` alike, through one shared construction helper (a per-site setting is exactly how the resumed path would silently keep the -32003 class). Direct machine embeddings (including the existing two-machine server-refusal test) keep the old -32010 -> ErrTurnActive -> -32003 mapping. With the flag on, a numeric-CodeConcurrency rejection triggers one stop, a bounded wait for clear evidence, and one retry; a second rejection returns `ErrOrphanedBackend`, which the front maps to a stable non-retryable -32603 message. Clear evidence must be explicitly session-scoped (`sessionId == sessID`): `ObserveEnvelope` is conn-wide, so a terminal or idle patch carrying a foreign or absent sessionId neither clears the wait nor resolves — the bound expires and the single retry still happens. A session-scoped terminal observed during the wait records its non-empty turnId as retired before the retry, so a late duplicate cannot bind (record-on-first-sight) or falsely complete the retried turn; the retried turn's own terminal resolves exactly once. The bounded wait doubles as a drain for a legitimate post-completion backend latch (the desktop's ~2 s window), so a quick successive prompt heals through the same path.
- Existing-test supersession: `internal/acpfront/cancel_test.go`'s `testBackendRefusalRejection` currently asserts that a slot-free backend -32010 refusal surfaces -32003. That exact shape is the orphan case under this contract, so its assertion is superseded. It is replaced by the front-tier orphan test; the legitimate local-busy -32003 surface remains locked by the `testFrontSlotRejection` arm and by the machine-tier `TestDoubleSendRejectedServerSide` (default OrphanRecovery=false). The `send-refused-32010` corpus row and its byte golden stay untouched as a fake-tier wire fixture.

Out of scope: zcode-cli provider 429 retry-policy changes; resume-capability advertisement; Orca UI changes; any change to the wire vocabulary, the ACP error shapes, or existing corpus JSON/goldens.
